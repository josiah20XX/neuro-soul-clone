# Summary

Soul Clone is a personal research project exploring the idea of a digital reflection of a person. Rather than trying to replace someone or make decisions for them, it is designed to act like a thoughtful mirror that helps a person reflect on how they think, feel, and behave.

The project aims to bring together different kinds of personal information, such as mood, habits, behavior, physical signals, and written reflections, to create a more personalized model of a person’s inner patterns. This can be useful for thinking through major decisions, comparing possible futures, or rehearsing difficult conversations in a safe environment.

At its core, Soul Clone is meant to support self-understanding, not certainty. It is designed to help someone ask questions like: “Why do I react this way?” “What might I feel in this situation?” or “How might my choices change under pressure?” The goal is not to predict the whole person perfectly, but to offer a useful, bounded way to explore possibilities.

The project also places strong emphasis on safety and ethics. It is designed to respect personal consent, avoid making real-world decisions on a person’s behalf, and pause when a situation becomes too intense or emotionally risky. The idea is to create a tool for reflection and planning while keeping human judgment and professional care in control.

In simple terms, Soul Clone is a research effort to build a personal “thinking companion” that helps people understand themselves better, explore future scenarios, and make decisions with more awareness—without pretending to be a complete replacement for the person themselves.
